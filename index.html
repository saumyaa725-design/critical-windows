      Concrete impact

      Priority Plan for Real Life

      The goal is not fear. The goal is knowing what to do first, especially when time, money, transportation, food access, or support are limited.

      If planning pregnancy

      • Start a prenatal vitamin with folic acid before pregnancy if possible.
      • Review medications and supplements with a clinician before changing anything.
      • Check home/work exposure risks: lead, smoke, solvents, pesticides, and high-mercury fish.
      • Build low-cost staples: beans, lentils, eggs if eaten, fortified cereal, frozen vegetables, yogurt or fortified alternatives.

      If already pregnant and surprised

      • Start prenatal care as soon as possible, even if some weeks have passed.
      • Begin a prenatal vitamin if tolerated; ask about folic acid, iron, iodine, and choline.
      • Do not panic about past exposures. Bring details to a clinician: timing, amount, and frequency.
      • If food is hard to access, ask about WIC, SNAP, food pantries, school/community programs, and clinic social work.

      If using alcohol, opioids, nicotine, or other drugs

      • Contact a clinician quickly and honestly. Medical support can reduce risk.
      • Do not stop opioids suddenly without medical guidance because withdrawal can be dangerous.
      • Ask about medication-assisted treatment, cessation support, mental health care, and harm reduction.
      • Bring the simulator output as a conversation starter, not as a diagnosis.

      If vegetarian, allergic, or low budget

      • Prioritize iron, B12, iodine, choline, protein, calcium, vitamin D, and DHA alternatives.
      • Use fortified foods when possible: plant milks, cereals, nutritional yeast, iodized salt, and prenatal vitamins.
      • Ask about safe supplements rather than guessing doses.
      • Choose affordable nutrient-dense foods that fit your restrictions instead of chasing expensive wellness products.

      Evidence model

      What the Simulator Is Based On

      The model favors prevention, harm reduction, and clinician conversation. It separates established risks from areas where evidence is still developing.

      Strong evidence signals

      • Alcohol can harm fetal development, and there is no known safe amount or safe time during pregnancy.
      • Opioid use disorder in pregnancy needs medical care; sudden stopping without guidance can be unsafe.
      • Fish guidance is a risk-benefit issue: pregnant people are advised to choose low-mercury seafood while avoiding high-mercury fish.
      • Pregnancy raises needs for nutrients such as folate, iron, iodine, choline, calcium, vitamin D, and others.

      Emerging or context-dependent areas

      • Cannabis evidence is still developing, but professional guidance advises cessation during pregnancy and screening through conversation.
      • Nutrition may modify vulnerability to some exposures, but exact individual outcomes cannot be predicted by a simple model.
      • Social determinants such as food access, housing, transportation, stigma, and healthcare access shape real risk and real solutions.
